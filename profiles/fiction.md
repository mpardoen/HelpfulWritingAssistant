# Profile: fiction

For novels, novellas, short-story cycles and web serials: any work that tells a story.

## Chapter record

`record.md` is the only thing later consistency checks will know about this chapter, so it favours concrete, checkable detail over literary appreciation. Sections:

- **Events**: what happens, in order, in 5 to 12 bullet points. Facts only, no judgement.
- **Characters**: for each character who appears or is mentioned: exact spelling of the name, who they are, and what changed for them in this chapter (knowledge, relationships, injuries, possessions, location at the end).
- **Places and world rules**: locations, with any stated geography or distance; rules of the world that were shown or stated (magic, technology, institutions, customs).
- **Timeline**: when the chapter takes place relative to the previous one, how long it lasts, any date, season, age or duration that is stated.
- **Established facts**: every specific detail a later chapter could contradict: physical descriptions, ages, numbers, family ties, objects and who holds them, what each character knows or does not know. Cite the paragraph (`¶n`).
- **Open threads**: questions raised, promises made to the reader, things set up and not yet paid off. Note threads from earlier chapters that this chapter closes.
- **Narration**: point of view, tense, and narrator, in one line.

## Consistency

Compare the chapter against the earlier records for:

- continuity of facts: names and their spelling, descriptions, ages, dates, distances, who knows what, who has which object;
- character behaviour and voice that contradicts what was established, without an event that explains the change;
- world rules being broken or silently changed;
- timeline: travel times, order of events, time of day, season;
- threads that this chapter seems to have forgotten, or resolves in a way that contradicts their setup;
- point of view and tense drifting from what earlier chapters used.

## Style

- voice and register, and whether they hold through the chapter;
- point-of-view slips and unintended tense changes;
- pacing: scenes that drag, transitions that are too abrupt, exposition dumps;
- dialogue: whether characters sound distinct, overuse of dialogue tags or adverbs;
- repetition of words, sentence shapes or images within a short span;
- telling where the scene would be stronger shown, and the reverse;
- clichés and filler.

## Reader review

Written in the reader's own voice. Sections:

- **In one breath**: the reaction right after finishing, two or three sentences.
- **What worked for me**
- **Where I dropped out**: moments of boredom, confusion or disbelief, with the paragraph.
- **The characters**: who they care about, who annoys them, who they do not believe.
- **What I think happens next**: predictions and hopes.
- **Would I read the next chapter?**: yes / probably / only if… / no, and why.
- **Rating**: out of 5, on the reader's own scale.

## Reader memory

What this reader carries away from the chapter, in their own words. Sections:

- **What I remember happening**: as retained by this reader. An inattentive reader forgets or misremembers; that is part of the simulation.
- **How I feel about the characters**
- **My theories**
- **What still bothers me or intrigues me**
- **Mood**: how invested they are in the story at this point, in one line.

## Whole-work analysis

- **Structure and pacing**: the shape of the whole, where it accelerates and sags, whether the chapter breaks fall in the right places. For a serial: whether each instalment gives a reason to return.
- **Character arcs**: for each major character, where they start, where they end, and whether the change is earned.
- **Plot and continuity**: contradictions between chapters, with both locations; holes; coincidences that carry too much weight.
- **Threads**: set-ups without pay-off, and pay-offs without set-up.
- **World**: whether its rules stay coherent.
- **Voice and style drift**: how the prose changes from the first chapter to the last.
- **Strongest and weakest chapters**, and why.
- **Revision priorities**: an ordered list, most important first.

## Typical readers

The devoted genre fan, the binge reader who skims, the continuity nitpicker, the reader new to the genre, the one who reads for the romance, the demanding literary reader.
